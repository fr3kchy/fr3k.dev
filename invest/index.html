00 // THE THESIS

One portfolio. Multiple ways to win.

FR3KD3V is not a single monolithic application. It is a portfolio of product lines developed around practical AI, software, hardware and operational problems. A customer can adopt one product without buying the entire ecosystem.

01

Independent products

Each platform has a distinct buyer, use case, deployment boundary and commercial test. Customers can start with one product and keep it useful on its own.

02

Optional integration

Versioned adapters connect products where they create value — without forcing every customer into one cloud, one database or one release cycle.

03

Local-first economics

Keep a genuinely useful self-hosted core. Monetise managed hosting, coordination, support, specialist workflows, premium content and business-grade operation.

04

Evidence before scale

Release claims are tied to source, tests and acceptance gates. A repository, successful build or heartbeat is not treated as proof of production readiness.

FOUNDER / OPERATOR

Freek (fR3k) — independent AI systems architect and operator. Founder-led product direction is paired with explicit engineering evidence, scoped delivery and customer acceptance.

THE COMMERCIAL FLYWHEEL

Demonstrate a real outcome → attract a qualified user → convert a pilot or subscription → retain through reliable delivery → introduce a relevant adjacent product → measure expansion. The expected ripple effect is a hypothesis to test, not a guaranteed viral multiplier.

What counts as recurring revenue?

MRR is active paying accounts multiplied by the realised recurring monthly price. ARR is MRR multiplied by twelve. Report contracted, invoiced, collected and recognised revenue separately.

Exclude setup fees, hardware resale, internal transfers, free trials and doubtful unpaid invoices from MRR. Allocate bundle revenue across products and count external revenue once.

What must be measured?

Provider inference, hosting, storage, transaction fees, support and fulfilment; gross margin; refunds and discounts; churn and retention; customer concentration; acquisition cost; support hours and cash collection.

Current gross margin, CAC, LTV, churn, burn rate and runway are not established in the available planning record.

03 // HOW CUSTOMERS PAY

A product-led subscription model

The commercial model is designed to keep adoption low-friction while charging for ongoing value. It is a proposed portfolio operating model; a single, fully integrated billing and entitlement service has not been verified across all products.

A

Free local / self-hosted core

Where practical, users can run useful software on their own devices or infrastructure. This builds trust, avoids mandatory cloud dependence and gives technical customers control over their data.

B

Paid subscriptions

Recurring plans unlock premium features, hosted access, collaboration, managed coordination, specialist workflows, premium content or business support. Each product can set its own tiers and limits.

C

Usage and managed services

Where costs vary, provider usage, compute, storage or processing can be metered with explicit limits. Setup, hardware, integration and bespoke delivery are quoted separately from MRR.

D

Optional bundles and expansion

Customers may add adjacent products or a bundle when there is a clear operational benefit. Bundle income is allocated across modules so portfolio revenue is never counted twice.

  1. DiscoverA demo, referral, technical release or product community introduces a specific use case.
  2. AdoptThe customer installs a free core, starts a bounded trial or agrees a paid pilot.
  3. ActivateOnboarding reaches one measurable outcome, with permissions and cost limits explicit.
  4. SubscribeThe customer pays for recurring features, hosting, support, content or managed operation.
  5. Retain & expandUsage, support quality and renewal evidence determine whether another module is relevant.

The proposed shared commercial contracts include optional entitlement checks and provider-cost usage, but local/self-hosted operation must not depend on central billing or identity. Payment processing, subscription lifecycle and entitlement enforcement require product-by-product implementation and acceptance.

04 // PLATFORM ARCHITECTURE

Modular by design. Governed at the edges.

Shared contracts reduce repeated engineering while preserving independent products. Integrations are incremental and versioned; the architecture does not require a forced monolith.

Independent product boundaries

Each platform remains separately installable, testable, versioned and useful. A failure in an optional adapter must not corrupt the standalone product.

One authoritative job ledger

For coordinated work, use stable job IDs, dependencies, idempotency keys, leases, fencing tokens and immutable terminal results. Workers operate with scoped authority.

Evidence as a first-class record

Preserve source references, timestamps, versions, hashes, reproduction steps and uncertainty labels. Keep raw customer evidence in approved restricted storage.

Explicit authority boundaries

Separate OBSERVE, AUTO, APPROVAL and PHYSICAL authority. Discovery is not permission; sensitive actions require approval and hardware changes require explicit physical authority.

OBSERVE→VERIFY→RECONCILE→IMPROVE→TEST→RECORD

Architecture is the adopted direction and implementation contract. It is not a claim that every product has already been integrated or that every shared service is production-ready.

Capital buys proof, distribution and capacity.

Any investment should accelerate a small number of measurable bottlenecks, not fund thirteen simultaneous launches. The final budget and allocation depend on the raise size, investor fit and diligence findings.

01

Product completion

Close end-to-end acceptance for the first release wave: Operations, Overlays and Fleet. Fund testing, packaging, deployment, backup/restore and documented limitations.

02

Commercial infrastructure

Implement onboarding, billing and entitlement workflows where justified, usage metering, customer support, renewal handling and revenue reconciliation.

Security & reliability

Strengthen tenant isolation, secrets, permission revocation, audit trails, recovery, cost ceilings and independent review of sensitive workflows.

Customer acquisition

08 // RISKS & DILIGENCE

Risks are part of the investment case.

The portfolio is ambitious. The execution model is designed to make its constraints visible early rather than conceal them behind aggregate projections.

Commercial demand is unproven

Validate each buyer, price, conversion rate and renewal through paid pilots and observed cohorts.

Portfolio focus can dilute delivery

Limit work in progress; sequence launches by readiness and buyer evidence, not by the number of ideas.

Bundles can inflate revenue

Maintain one external revenue ledger and allocate bundle receipts across products exactly once.

Infrastructure costs can erode margin

Meter inference, hosting, storage and support; apply quotas and customer-visible limits.

Founder support can become a bottleneck

Assign support ownership, document onboarding and cap customer scope to available capacity.

Security, rights and compliance matter

Gate releases on isolation, revocation, recovery, source rights and appropriate specialist review.

High-consequence product claims need independent review

No unsupported HR/payroll compliance, calibrated RF accuracy, emergency communications reliability, predictive simulation or scientific/medical claims. Specialist evidence and review are required before making them.

Open diligence items

  • Current revenue, invoices, contracts, costs, cash position and runway.
  • Legal entity, ownership structure, cap table and intellectual-property assignments.
  • Canonical repository mapping, licences, third-party dependencies and product-by-product acceptance.
  • Customer pipeline, pilot evidence, retention, pricing validation and support capacity.
  • Privacy, security, insurance and jurisdiction-specific compliance review.
  • Final raise amount, instrument, valuation, equity allocation and use-of-funds budget.
